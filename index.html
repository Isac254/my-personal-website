About Me

Hello, and welcome to my portfolio!

I am a Bachelor of Science in Geography student at Egerton University with a growing interest in Geographic Information Systems (GIS), land surveying, digital mapping, and spatial data analysis. I am passionate about understanding the world through geographic information and exploring how modern geospatial technologies can help address real-world challenges.

My academic journey has helped me appreciate the relationship between people, the environment, and the places we live in. Through my studies, I am developing knowledge in GIS, cartography, remote sensing, and environmental management, while also exploring the practical side of surveying and geographic data collection.

I am particularly interested in how GIS and land surveying complement each other. Surveying provides important measurements and positional information about features on the ground, while GIS helps organize, analyze, visualize, and interpret geographic data. Combining these approaches can support mapping, infrastructure planning, environmental studies, and informed decision-making.

I am developing practical skills in ArcMap, GPS data collection, GNSS surveying using base and rover receivers, Total Station operations, and AutoCAD. I am also interested in remote sensing, spatial analysis, and the use of digital tools to produce reliable and meaningful geographic information.

One of my current projects involves assessing the distribution and condition of street lights at Egerton University Main Campus. This project allows me to explore how field data collection and GIS mapping can help identify infrastructure patterns and areas that may require attention.

Beyond the classroom, I value curiosity, teamwork, continuous learning, and practical experience. I look forward to strengthening my technical abilities, learning from other professionals, and contributing to projects that make meaningful use of geographic and spatial information.

My ambition is to grow into a capable geospatial professional who combines geographical knowledge, surveying techniques, and GIS technology to turn field measurements and geographic data into useful information.

FEATURED PROJECT
🗺️

GIS Assessment of Street Lights

Egerton University Main Campus

This project focuses on mapping the locations of existing street lights and assessing their condition to understand their spatial distribution across the campus.

Project Objectives

  • Map the geographic locations of street lights.
  • Record their condition and operational status.
  • Examine their distribution in relation to roads and campus infrastructure.
  • Identify potential gaps in lighting coverage.

Tools and Techniques

ArcMap GPS Excel Field Data Collection Spatial Analysis

Expected Outputs

A GIS map and organized spatial database showing street-light locations and recorded conditions, helping highlight areas that may need further investigation, maintenance, or improved lighting.

Project status: Academic project
